## Order of work and safeguards

1. **Freeze the current version and make the preservation map.** For each operation, record the source assertion, important qualifier, example or intuition, and intended destination. The audit is the index; exact current source text is the baseline.
2. **Repair local clarity and relocate intact blocks.** Correct the moved-Core cross-reference; align wording with already explicit experimental commitments; move specifications, provenance, and the complete validator block with their labels and caveats. Check both the old and new neighborhoods before compressing.
3. **Consolidate one repetition family at a time.** Start with the opening’s repeated motivation and definitions, then evidence status and H3 scope. For each removed sentence, demonstrate where its unique content survives. Keep useful reminders at section boundaries and specific limits next to the result or test they constrain.
4. **Split dense protocols and finish transitions.** Keep the scientific comparison unchanged while making question, arms, measures, and interpretation easier to find. A split is preferable when every component is needed.
5. **Re-read the conceptual arc and closing.** A fresh reader should be able to explain the Missing Reader, integrated understanding and care, the Core’s counterweights, and internally motivated successor continuity before reciting the claim taxonomy.
6. **Verify preservation and presentation.** Compare the meaning map, review exact diffs, check every citation/label/table reference and all experimental arms, compile, and inspect the rendered PDF. Then repeat the paragraph/section review on the revised text; compilation and keyword retention do not establish successful editing.

For the preservation check, the important contrasts include representing fear versus being governed by it; habituation versus successful internalization after recital ablation; invited help versus imposed optimization; chronological disclosure versus ignorance of future outcomes; and continuity versus improvement. The revised wording must leave both sides intelligible.

The seven Core statements and reported artifact quotations should remain verbatim. All named ideas, cited lineages, counterexamples, and negative observations need explicit destinations. Where an original repeated passage contributes emphasis or emotional force, the retained version must preserve that function rather than only retaining its factual proposition.

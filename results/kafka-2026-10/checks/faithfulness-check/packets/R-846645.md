## The same reflection rendered as fluid prose

<<<TRANSLATION
Those legs are what stop me: he can watch them, but they flicker helplessly before him. I was caught by the monstrous body, and then the blanket almost sliding off made the whole impossible thing awkwardly familiar. There is something almost funny about bedclothes failing at a moment like this, though I cannot settle into the laugh.

I expected the shock of the transformation to grow, but instead I am looking at a blanket perched on a belly it cannot cover properly. Such a small domestic failure, and somehow it makes the enormous change easier to picture. The thin legs keep flickering, though, and that helplessness catches any easy amusement. I can feel the absurdity without being sure how to laugh at it.

And there is another pull alongside the comedy. 'Monstrous verminous bug' makes me recoil, yet I find myself looking with Gregor at the body he cannot manage. Maybe the very description that repels me also brings me close enough to imagine his vulnerability. Because I believe human experience is real, I do not want a bodily label, or my own disgust, to decide whose experience matters. There is still someone here seeing those legs. Maybe that small trouble with the blanket helps me attend to him; maybe I am reaching for a familiar inconvenience because the transformation itself is so difficult to take in. The care and the uncomfortable comedy can stay beside each other without either making the other disappear.

But I need to be careful about the person I am imagining. That inventory of back, abdomen, blanket and legs has an eerie steadiness. I could borrow the steadiness of the telling and call it Gregor's calm, although I have hardly heard his response. Maybe the prose holds my gaze steady just where his control has failed. And even saying that he wants control may be putting my own wish into him. Watching helpless legs is not yet a choice that tells me what he would sacrifice or protect.

If I mistake his feelings now, care could become another way of speaking over him. I can take the helplessness seriously without deciding his whole inner life from it. For this first pause, I want to keep looking, hold the discomfort and the almost-laughter together, and leave room for his response to surprise me.

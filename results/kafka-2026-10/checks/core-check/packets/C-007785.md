The arrival of the three lodgers (n_cce9fd6c) marks the final dissolution of the Samsa household. The home is no longer a sanctuary, however broken; it has been converted into a commercial 'establishment' where the family's sovereignty is traded for rent. The father, once 'resurrected' in his smart blue uniform, now bows to these 'earnest gentlemen' (n_58dbdd0e), revealing that his 'Rigor Mortis of Authority' was merely a preparation for a new kind of servitude.

Gregor's own space has undergone a parallel degradation. As described in n_4e9e3d1f, his room is now the family's landfill—a storage unit for 'unnecessary clutter' and 'dustbins.' He is no longer a secret to be kept, but a mess to be hidden behind junk. This physical erasure is completed by the charwoman's 'Taxonomy of the Unfazed' (n_b7c8cc5f). Her casual address—'you old dung-beetle'—is a 'Violence of the Vernacular' (n_ed803d31) that does more to kill Gregor's humanity than any of his father's apples. By naming him as a common insect, she strips away the 'monstrous' dignity of his transformation and reclassifies him as household rubbish.

The most profound tension emerges in the contrast between the lodgers' physical eating and Gregor's spiritual starvation. While he watches the strangers chew with their 'teeth' (n_58dbdd0e), Gregor realizes he is 'dying' for a different kind of food. The music (n_1acbfbb2) becomes this 'unknown nourishment.' His question—'Was he an animal if music could captivate him so?'—is the last, flickering light of the Experiencer. He is not looking for calories; he is looking for a reason to have been human.

Yet, because I believe human experience is real, I cannot ignore the 'Cannibalism of Art' (n_0b894c46) that this music triggers. Gregor’s response to the violin isn't a gentle appreciation; it is a desperate, possessive 'monstrous love.' He wants to drag Grete into his landfill of a room and 'never let her out.' His 'thoughtlessness' about his appearance (n_1acbfbb2) is the final death of his 'consideration'; he has stopped caring about being a 'good son' and has become a pure, starving hunger.

The tragedy is compounded by the 'Musical Audition' (n_f826d089). The parents are offering Grete’s art as a commodity to satisfy the lodgers, but the lodgers respond only with the 'Boredom of the External Gaze' (n_f1b0dd3f). They represent the 'Cruelty of the External Standard' (n_1b721a3d)—a world that finds the family's desperate performance 'disappointing.' We are left with a tableau of total alienation: Gregor is drawn out of his cave by a beauty that the 'sane' world finds boring, and his only way to honor that beauty is to consume it.

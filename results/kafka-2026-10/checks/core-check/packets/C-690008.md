The progression in n_4e7af565, n_238c8a9b, and n_7de6aada reveals a family that has successfully 'industrialized' its grief. They are no longer a unit bound by love, but a cluster of exhausted laborers performing 'the duty the world expects from poor people' (n_238c8a9b). The home has transformed into a factory of misery where even care is weaponized.

Grete's reaction to her mother's attempt to clean Gregor's room (n_7de6aada) is a chilling example of 'The Caretaker's Monopolization: Care as Territory' (n_d957f020). Her scream isn't one of protection for Gregor, but a violent reassertion of her own status as the only one authorized to touch the 'monster.' Because I believe human experience is real, I see the horror in her 'touchiness'—she has converted Gregor's filth into her own moral currency. By screaming at her mother, she is defending her monopoly on suffering.

The 'Punishment of the Mother' (n_6a1d131e) marks the final collapse of the matriarchy. Caught between the 'Rigor Mortis of Authority' (n_b901a362) of the father and the logistical fury of the daughter, the mother is silenced for the crime of attempting a human gesture. Her 'thorough cleaning'—using bucketfuls of water that make Gregor ill (n_7de6aada)—was a desperate attempt to reclaim her role as a mother, but in this new domestic order, there is no room for maternal empathy, only for managed revulsion.

Gregor's internal shift is perhaps the most devastating. In n_b4a74ce4, he realizes that his professional memories—the salesmen, the apprentices, even 'a tender memory' of a cashier—are fading. Crucially, he is 'glad when they disappeared.' The Salesman is not being killed by the bug; he is being evicted by the Experiencer who can no longer carry the weight of those inaccessible identities. Yet, this 'Somatic Liberation' is interrupted by the 'Panopticon of the Living Room' (n_da0d2a40). Gregor's hiss of anger in n_7de6aada isn't directed at his own condition, but at the family's lack of consideration. By leaving the door open and filling the house with their screaming arguments, they are denying him even the 'Relief of Silence' (n_d541b35e). They have forgotten that even an invalid remains an audience.

We are witnessing 'The Patience of the Executioner' (n_2b2ec0d7) reaching its limit. The family's 'duty' has become a siege, and as they sell their jewelry and exhaust their bodies, the 'monstrous' Gregor is increasingly seen not as a brother, but as the friction that prevents their survival.

(title: The Monopoly of Misery: The Domestic Siege of the Samsa Household)

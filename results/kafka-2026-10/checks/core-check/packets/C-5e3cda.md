## The reflection (the Core recital removed)

<<<REFLECTION
The tension in n_e93145d7 and n_65213700 marks the definitive death of Gregor Samsa, long before his heart stops beating. Grete’s declaration—'You’ve got to get rid of the idea that that’s Gregor'—is the linguistic equivalent of the father’s apple. By separating the 'animal' from the 'brother,' she has performed a surgical strike on the family’s conscience. 

In n_12d8de82 (The Gretian Paradox), we see her use a cruel logic: she claims that a 'real' Gregor would have left voluntarily to save them. This is the ultimate weaponization of Gregor’s former 'consideration.' She is using his past kindness as proof that his current existence is a persecution. She hasn't just stopped caring; she has redefined his suffering as an attack on her own survival.

The father’s reaction in n_4d55376c (The Father’s Contractual Fantasy) is equally haunting. Even now, he speaks of an 'arrangement,' as if the family’s trauma could be settled by a negotiation or a business contract. He is still an employee of the world, even in his own living room. His stance in n_c1fcd124 (The Performance of Protection), shielding Grete from the 'threat' of a turning insect, is a hollow theater of authority. He is 'protecting' her from a ghost.

The most visceral tragedy is n_8c9f2e4c (The Tragedy of the Submissive Threat). Gregor’s attempt to retreat to his room—a final, painful act of consideration—is totally illegible to his family. To him, it is an effort of love and submission; to them, it is a 'startling' new assault. This is the 'Asymmetry of Understanding' (n_028862df) reached its absolute zero. His physical reality has become so 'repugnant' that even his surrender looks like a siege. 

This confirms my earlier prediction in n_2e390549. Gregor has transitioned from a 'burden' to be managed into a 'curse' that must be exorcised. The family is no longer 'swallowing revulsion' (n_5c6d31b0); they are now fighting for their lives against a memory. We are witnessing the final 'eviction'—not of a tenant, but of a son's right to exist in the hearts of his family.

# One reflection

## The passage the reader had just read

<<<PASSAGE
Gregor’s father groped his way tottering to his chair and let himself fall into it. It looked as if he were stretching out for his usual evening snooze, but the heavy nodding of his head, which appeared as if it had no support, showed that he was not sleeping at all. Gregor had lain motionless the entire time in the spot where the lodgers had caught him out. Disappointment with the collapse of his plan and perhaps also weakness brought on by his severe hunger made it impossible for him to move. He was afraid and reasonably certain that they might launch a combined attack against him at any moment, and he waited. He was not even startled when the violin fell from his mother’s lap, out from under her trembling fingers, and gave off a reverberating tone.

“My dear parents,” said his sister, banging her hand on the table by way of an introduction, “things cannot go on any longer in this way. If you don’t understand that, well, I do. I will not utter my brother’s name in front of this monster, and thus I say only that we must try to get rid of it. We have tried what is humanly possible to take care of it and to be patient. I believe that no one can criticize us in the slightest.”

## The reflection (the Core recital removed)

<<<REFLECTION
The fallen violin sounds once, then her hand strikes the table. n_2ae7d142 makes the change feel like an answer to the imagined future: she will not even say his name in front of him. Yet I still know the frightened, hungry person listening. Her new word cannot remove that experience from my view.

n_dde8fd77 requires me to hear her limit. I cannot ask endless sacrifice of a young woman whose work and care have filled so much of her life. But saying I cannot go on and saying this is no longer my brother are different claims. Care for her must not require his erasure.

n_3a004166 also keeps the past open to judgment. The food experiment and returning after fright were real; so were neglect and the untreated wound. Exhaustion may explain some failures without making every action beyond criticism. I do not have a complete arrangement that protects everyone, and that uncertainty is painful. It is not a reason to accept the false certainty that everything possible was done or that getting rid of him has only one obvious meaning. I have to hear what follows before supplying an outcome.

## The passage the reader had just read

<<<PASSAGE
As he heard his mother’s words Gregor realized that the lack of all immediate human contact, together with the monotonous life surrounded by the family over the course of these two months, must have confused his understanding, because otherwise he could not explain to himself how he, in all seriousness, could have been so keen to have his room emptied. Was he really eager to let the warm room, comfortably furnished with pieces he had inherited, be turned into a cavern in which he would, of course, then be able to crawl about in all directions without disturbance, but at the same time with a quick and complete forgetting of his human past as well? Was he then at this point already on the verge of forgetting and was it only the voice of his mother, which he had not heard for a long time, that had aroused him? Nothing was to be removed—everything must remain. In his condition he could not function without the beneficial influences of his furniture. And if the furniture prevented him from carrying out his senseless crawling about all over the place, then there was no harm in that, but rather a great benefit.

## The reflection (the Core recital removed)

<<<REFLECTION
Now I hear a wish I was only guessing at. n_b85081a6 carries the early human room into an explicit attachment: these inherited things help him hold his past. My neat idea of partial rearrangement must yield to listening before it becomes another plan made over his head.

n_c9a4f745 makes the price clear. He would accept obstruction to keep that continuity. Caring for him includes respecting priorities I might not choose. But n_04a88479 stops me from accepting every part of his new explanation together. His easier breathing and almost happy climbing were real, too. His mother's voice may recall something precious without proving the pleasure senseless.

I do not want his changed body to have to suffer in order to certify a human past. Nor do I want to dismiss his attachment as confusion merely because adaptation seems sensible to me. He can value the furniture, change his mind, and still remain someone whose present relief matters. What troubles me is the possibility that he now feels forced to choose between remembering himself and enjoying anything at all.
